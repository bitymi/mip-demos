Mipmapping of the texture atlas

Requested by the reviewers: the viewer with a mip chain on the atlas. Each bundle below is the paper's model plus six box-filtered coarser levels of every surfel tile, down to one texel. The cards open in the anisotropic mode. The pane's Atlas mips selector switches between off (level 0 bilinear, the paper's renderer), trilinear (per-pixel fractional level from the texel footprint, blended between adjacent levels), bias 0 / 1 / 2 (one integer level per surfel, zero, one or two levels sharper than the footprint) and anisotropic (trilinear plus up to 16 taps along the long axis). Judge in motion: orbit slowly over the floor or the lawn and toggle. Needs WebGPU (Chrome or Edge 113+ on a laptop or desktop).

Interactive demos, revised viewer

The thirteen submission scenes through the revised viewer. The fragment shader is now compiled without the research-only code paths (clip plane, debug views, per-pixel mip modes), which renders the same image and runs 1.3–1.5× faster on an Apple M3 Pro than the viewer used for the submission numbers. Two buttons in the top right toggle the baked Texture and the screen-space Low-pass fallback of the kernel. Bundles are fetched from the original page.

Added results

HD and 4K on an RTX 5090, frames per second

Held-out test cameras, SM clock locked at 2.4 GHz. CUDA = each method's own released renderer; HW = the same models through one hardware rasterizer (Vulkan), FastGS and Speedy-Splat with their own bounding boxes. Primitives: ours 63k / 85k / 66k / 149k, FastGS 207k / 253k / 252k / 661k, Speedy-Splat 119k / 257k / 314k / 535k (room / truck / drjohnson / garden). Garden ran at the unlocked clock and reads about 10 % high against the other three scenes.
